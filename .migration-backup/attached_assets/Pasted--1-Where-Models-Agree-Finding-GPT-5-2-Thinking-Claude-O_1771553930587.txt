### 4. Comprehensive Analysis

**High-Confidence Findings**  
All three models converge on the same answer for buildings: **use a stamp/template library** and place those templates into the generated terrain with simple constraints (no overlap, valid ground, spacing rules, biome weighting). This is the most reliable way to get buildings that feel like “real places” (hospital wing, gas station, farmhouse) without having to invent a full procedural architecture engine on day one. It also matches your game’s needs: survival horror relies on *recognizable* spaces, chokepoints, and readable interiors—templates deliver that immediately. [ppl-ai-file-upload.s3.amazonaws](https://ppl-ai-file-upload.s3.amazonaws.com/web/direct-files/collection_a53234d6-2c3d-4491-8555-5ab7b12ae0c4/c3f6c13c-4855-4efe-bd65-300e0ae1b158/hello-i-finished-python-should-U.z59vDWQ0CHnjpMvNup4A.md)

They also agree that your buildings should not be “just walls.” Templates should embed gameplay hooks: **door locations**, **loot sockets**, and **cover clusters** (tables/crates/beds) so that combat, scavenging, and objectives naturally emerge from structure layout rather than random noise. This directly helps your AI too: the Brain can reason about “go to door,” “take cover,” “search loot socket” using stable, named map affordances instead of interpreting chaotic geometry. [ppl-ai-file-upload.s3.amazonaws](https://ppl-ai-file-upload.s3.amazonaws.com/web/direct-files/collection_a53234d6-2c3d-4491-8555-5ab7b12ae0c4/c3f6c13c-4855-4efe-bd65-300e0ae1b158/hello-i-finished-python-should-U.z59vDWQ0CHnjpMvNup4A.md)

**Areas of Divergence**  
The main difference is how far to push generation sophistication after you ship the first version. GPT-5.2 Thinking provides a clear upgrade path (stamps first, then optionally “shell + fill,” then room-grammar, and only much later WFC). Claude Opus 4.6 and Gemini 3.1 Pro are basically saying: don’t overcomplicate—**stamps alone** can already give you huge mileage, especially when you add rotations, ruined variants, and randomized dressing. Practically: for WSS2, stamps are both the “best now” and likely “good enough for a long time.” [ppl-ai-file-upload.s3.amazonaws](https://ppl-ai-file-upload.s3.amazonaws.com/web/direct-files/collection_a53234d6-2c3d-4491-8555-5ab7b12ae0c4/c3f6c13c-4855-4efe-bd65-300e0ae1b158/hello-i-finished-python-should-U.z59vDWQ0CHnjpMvNup4A.md)

Another divergence is what to pay attention to as you commit to sub-tile entities. Claude’s warning is important: if you go sub-tile, make sure **weapon range, melee checks, noise radius, and cover evaluation** use float positions (or at least tile + intra-tile offset), otherwise the game will still *feel* tile-locked even though entities render smoothly. That’s not a building-gen issue, but it will affect how building interiors play (doorway fights, corner peeks, swarming in rooms). **Unique Insights Worth Noting**  
GPT’s “tile layers” tip is genuinely a best practice for your codebase: treat the world as layered data (terrain vs structure vs props vs sockets). Then systems like `CoverSystem` and `NoiseSystem` can query the board cleanly without caring how a building got generated. Gemini’s point about cover being meaningful is the design-side version of the same thing: stamps let you intentionally create “good fights” (a desk near a window; a hallway choke), which is hard with purely random placement.
